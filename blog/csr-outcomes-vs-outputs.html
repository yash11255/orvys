Outcomes vs Outputs: Measuring What Your CSR Actually Changed

Most CSR reports count activities. The best measure change in people's lives. A practical guide to outcome indicators for common CSR programmes.

Orvys Research Team · 15 July 2026 · 5 min read

"We distributed 10,000 kits." "We trained 2,000 youth." Most CSR reports are full of numbers like these. They are outputs: what the programme did. Boards and communities increasingly want to know the outcomes: what changed as a result.

The results chain

  1. Inputs: money, staff and materials.
  2. Activities: training, distribution and construction.
  3. Outputs: the direct products, such as people trained or centres built.
  4. Outcomes: changes in knowledge, behaviour, health or income.
  5. Impact: long-term change in wellbeing at population level.

Outcome indicators by theme

ThemeOutput (what we did)Outcome (what changed)
NutritionAnganwadis equippedChange in stunting, wasting and anaemia rates
EducationChildren enrolled in reading campsShare of children reading at grade level
HealthPatients screenedShare diagnosed and on treatment
SkillingYouth trainedShare employed after 12 months; earnings
WaterPlants installedHouseholds using safe water; plants still working after 3 years
ResilienceShelters builtLosses and recovery time after floods

How to get there

  • Measure a baseline before starting. Without it, change cannot be shown.
  • Keep indicators few and meaningful: three good outcome measures beat thirty activity counts.
  • Collect data regularly, not only at the end, so the programme can correct course.
  • Use comparisons with similar villages or schools where possible.
  • Commission independent evaluation for larger programmes (see impact assessment rules).

Why it matters

Outcome measurement is not only for reports. It tells a company which programmes to scale, which to fix and which to stop, and turns CSR from spending into investment. Every Orvys programme is built around outcome indicators from day one. See how we work.
